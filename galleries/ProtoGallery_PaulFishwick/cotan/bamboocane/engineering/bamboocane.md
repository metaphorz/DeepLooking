Bamboo is one of humanity's oldest and most versatile engineering materials. Its tensile strength (roughly 370 MPa) rivals mild steel, while its compressive strength exceeds that of concrete. In Cotán's era, bamboo and cane were used for construction, irrigation channels, musical instruments, writing implements, and — as shown here — food preparation tools.

The cane's use as a bird skewer exploits a specific engineering property: the smooth, hard outer surface (rich in silica) penetrates flesh easily, while the hollow interior keeps the tool light and easy to handle. The nodes provide natural grip points and prevent the birds from sliding.

Spanish cane (Arundo donax), native to the Mediterranean, was the likely species depicted. Unlike tropical bamboo, Arundo grows in dense stands along rivers and irrigation ditches throughout Castile, making it freely available to even the poorest households.
