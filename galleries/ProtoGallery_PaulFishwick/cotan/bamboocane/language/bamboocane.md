The Spanish word "caña" derives from Latin "canna" and Greek "kanna," ultimately from Sumerian "gin" — one of the oldest cultivated-plant words in any language, tracing an unbroken linguistic chain across 5,000 years of agricultural civilization.

In Golden Age Spanish, "caña" carried multiple meanings: a fishing rod, a reed pen for writing, a measure of length (approximately 2.8 meters), and the hollow stalk used here as a skewer for game birds. The word's versatility reflects the material's ubiquity in Mediterranean daily life.

The bamboo cane in Cotán's painting serves as a "espetón" — a spit or skewer. This practice of threading small birds onto a rigid cane was standard in Spanish markets, both for transport and display. The word "espetón" itself comes from "espeto" (to skewer), sharing its root with "espada" (sword).
