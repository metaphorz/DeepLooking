The six birds on the cane occupy a fundamentally different artistic space than the hanging pair above. While the hanging birds float in the void, these birds rest solidly on the stone ledge — they are earthbound, grounded, material.

Cotán paints the cane birds as a dense, compact mass. Individual species are harder to distinguish here than in the hanging pair; the birds overlap and compress against each other, their feathers mingling into a rich texture of browns, grays, and olive tones. The visual effect is of abundance — a market bundle ready for the kitchen.

The vertical bamboo cane creates a strong compositional line that anchors the left side of the painting. Against the dark void, the bird-and-cane assembly reads as a single sculptural form — a column of organic matter balanced between the bright quinces above and the root vegetables below. This vertical element echoes the ascending cardoon on the right, creating a frame-within-a-frame.
