The Museo del Prado identifies six birds on the cane: two European Serins ("verdecillos"), two European Goldfinches ("jilgueros"), and two House Sparrows ("gorriones"). Each name reveals something about the bird it describes.

"Verdecillo" means "little green one," a diminutive of "verde" (green), describing the serin's olive-streaked plumage. It is the smallest European finch — so small that two together weigh less than a single sparrow.

"Jilguero" comes from Latin "carduelis" (thistle-eater), connecting this bird to the cardoon in the same painting. The linguistic thread is literal: goldfinches feed on the seeds of the same plant family that produced the great vegetable arching on the right.

"Gorrión" derives from a pre-Roman substrate language, marking the sparrow as one of the oldest named birds on the Iberian Peninsula. The word's antiquity reflects the species' ancient bond with human habitation.
