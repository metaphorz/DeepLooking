Fresh snow reflects most of the sunlight that hits it, often 80 to 90 percent, while dark rock and forest reflect much less. Scientists call this reflecting power albedo. Because snow bounces sunlight back to space, it stays cold, and the cold keeps it from melting. That helps patches of snow survive on high, shaded slopes. When snow melts and dark rock is exposed, the ground absorbs more heat and melts the snow around it faster. Look at the edges of the white areas, where snow and rock meet.
