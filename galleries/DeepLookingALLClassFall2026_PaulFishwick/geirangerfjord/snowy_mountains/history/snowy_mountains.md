People in Norway have used skis for thousands of years, for hunting and travel across snowy ground. Modern skiing as a sport owes much to Sondre Norheim, born in 1825 in Morgedal in the Telemark region. He used bindings made of twisted willow that held the heel, and skis that were narrower in the middle, which made turning and jumping much easier. The Telemark turn is named after his home region. In 1884 he emigrated to North Dakota, as many Norwegians did. Snowy mountains like these shaped a whole culture of winter travel.
