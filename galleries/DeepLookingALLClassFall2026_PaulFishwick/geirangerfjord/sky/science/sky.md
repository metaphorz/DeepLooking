Moist air blowing in from the North Atlantic meets the mountains of western Norway and has nowhere to go but up. As it rises it cools, and cool air cannot hold as much water vapor, so the vapor condenses into tiny droplets and forms clouds. Meteorologists call this orographic lift. It explains why the clouds here sit low over the peaks and why the coastal mountains get so much rain and snow. On the far side of a mountain range, the air sinks and warms, often leaving a drier rain shadow.
