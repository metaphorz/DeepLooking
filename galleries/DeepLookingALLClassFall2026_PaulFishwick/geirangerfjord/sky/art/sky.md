Johan Christian Dahl, born in Bergen in 1788, is often called the father of Norwegian landscape painting. He worked in Dresden, where he was a friend of Caspar David Friedrich, but he kept returning to paint the mountains and fjords of western Norway. Dahl made many small cloud studies in oil, painted quickly outdoors to catch how light moved through a changing sky. Look at the clouds here: the thick grey layer above, the brighter band near the peaks, and the soft shadows they cast on the slopes.
