Modern weather forecasting owes a lot to Norway. Around 1918, the scientist Vilhelm Bjerknes and his team in Bergen studied how masses of warm and cold air meet. They called the boundary between them a front, borrowing the word from the battle lines of the First World War, which was being fought at the time. Their ideas, known as the Bergen School, are still used on every weather map that shows warm and cold fronts. The changing clouds over this fjord are the kind of weather they set out to explain.
