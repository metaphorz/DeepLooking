Road builders describe steepness as a grade, the rise divided by the run, written as a percent. A road that climbs 10 meters over 100 meters of distance has a 10 percent grade. The slopes of this valley are much steeper than any car could drive straight up, so the road winds back and forth. Each bend adds distance, which lowers the grade. If a road has to climb 600 meters and can only rise at 8 percent, how many meters long must it be at least? Look for the zigzag road on the slope above the fjord.
