The Eagle Road, Ørnevegen in Norwegian, climbs from Geiranger up the steep mountainside north of the fjord. It uses eleven hairpin bends to gain height, each one a sharp U-turn cut into the slope and held up by stone walls. It opened in 1955, and from the top there is a famous view down the fjord. Building roads like this means blasting rock, draining water away so the road does not wash out, and fitting each turn into a narrow ledge. The road at the bottom of this photo also winds to fit the land.
