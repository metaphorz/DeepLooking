On the steep walls of the Geirangerfjord, families once farmed tiny ledges hundreds of meters above the water. The best known is Skageflå, reached by a steep path from the fjord. People grew potatoes and kept goats and cows on these shelves of land, and children were sometimes tied with ropes so they would not fall. A local story says farmers pulled up their ladders when the tax collector came. These ledge farms were given up in the early 1900s, and today hikers visit them for the view.
