During the Ice Ages, thick glaciers flowed down from the mountains toward the sea. They ground into the rock and carved deep valleys with steep sides and flat bottoms, a U-shaped cross section rather than the V shape a river cuts. When the ice melted, about 10,000 years ago, the sea flowed in and filled the lower valleys. That is what a fjord is: a drowned glacial valley. Many fjords are deepest far inland and shallower at the mouth, where the glacier dropped rock. Look at how steeply the walls fall straight into the water.
