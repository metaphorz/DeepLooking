The Oseberg ship is a Viking ship built around the year 820 and buried in a mound in southern Norway as the grave of two women. It was dug up in 1904. Its prow curls up into a spiral shaped like a snake's head, and its sides are covered with carved animals twisting together in what experts call the gripping-beast style. To the Vikings, a ship was both a working machine and a work of art. Today's cruise ships are designed for comfort, but their sleek white shapes still say something about style and travel.
