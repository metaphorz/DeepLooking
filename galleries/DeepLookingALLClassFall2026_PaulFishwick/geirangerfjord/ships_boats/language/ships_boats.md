Many English sailing words came from the Norse world. Viking ships were steered with a large oar fixed to the right side near the back, so the right side became the steering board, steorbord in Old English, now starboard. The left side was kept clear for tying up at the dock, which is one reason it is called port. Keel comes from Old Norse kjölr, the backbone of a ship. When you look at the two white cruise ships here, you are looking at vessels described with words a Viking sailor would recognize.
