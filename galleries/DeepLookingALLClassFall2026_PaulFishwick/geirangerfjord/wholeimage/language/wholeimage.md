Norway has two official ways of writing Norwegian, Bokmål and Nynorsk. Nynorsk, meaning New Norwegian, was created by Ivar Aasen, born in 1813 in Ørsta, a district in Sunnmøre not far from Geiranger. Aasen was a farmer's son who taught himself languages. He walked from valley to valley, collecting the dialects of rural Norway, and built a written language from them. His grammar and dictionary came out around 1850. Many communities in western Norway, including the area around Geiranger, still use Nynorsk in schools and town signs.
