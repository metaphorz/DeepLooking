Look at how the mountains change as they get farther away. The near slopes are dark green with sharp detail, while the distant peaks look paler, bluer and softer. Painters call this aerial perspective. Air contains tiny particles and water droplets that scatter light, so the more air between you and an object, the more it fades toward the color of the sky. Leonardo da Vinci wrote about it, and landscape painters use it to create depth on a flat canvas. This photo shows it clearly, from the valley floor to the snowy ridge.
