Peter Lanyon, born in St Ives in 1918, was a Cornish painter who wanted to show what it felt like to be inside the landscape, not just looking at it. His paintings break fields, cliffs, mines and sea into bold shapes and colors. In 1959 he took up gliding so he could see Cornwall from above, and the patterns of fields and hedges became part of his art. Look at the patchwork of green fields on the hill here. Could you turn their shapes into a painting with no horizon at all?
