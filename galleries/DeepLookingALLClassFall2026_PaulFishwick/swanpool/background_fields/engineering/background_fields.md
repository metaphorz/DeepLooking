The fields in Cornwall are not divided by fences or by bushes alone. A Cornish hedge is a bank of earth faced on both sides with stones carefully fitted together, with grasses, flowers and shrubs growing on top. Builders lean the stone faces inward so the bank stays strong for hundreds of years. Some Cornish hedges are thousands of years old, and by some estimates there are around 30,000 miles of them in the county. The dark lines between the fields in Pamela's photo are likely hedges like these.
