A hedgerow is more than a boundary. It gives shelter, food and nesting places to birds, insects, small mammals and many kinds of wildflower. Because hedges link one patch of habitat to the next, they act as wildlife corridors, letting animals such as hedgehogs and dormice move safely across farmland. A single long hedge can hold dozens of plant species. Scientists sometimes estimate the age of a hedge by counting the woody species in a 30 meter stretch. The dark edges around these green fields may be some of the richest habitat in the view.
