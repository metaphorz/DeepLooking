Cornish, called Kernewek by its speakers, is a Celtic language closely related to Welsh and Breton. It was once spoken across Cornwall, but English slowly replaced it. Dolly Pentreath, who died in 1777, is often called the last native speaker, though others probably knew some Cornish after her. In the 1900s, people began to revive it from old texts and place names. In 2010, UNESCO changed its status from extinct to critically endangered. Today children can learn Cornish, and many names in this landscape still speak it.
