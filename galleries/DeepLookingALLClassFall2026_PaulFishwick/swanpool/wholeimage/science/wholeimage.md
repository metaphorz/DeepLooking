Cornwall is as far north as parts of Canada, yet its winters are mild and frost is uncommon near the sea. One reason is the North Atlantic Current, an extension of the Gulf Stream that carries warm water from the tropics across the ocean toward Europe. The warm sea warms the air above it, and westerly winds bring that air onshore. This is why palms and other subtropical plants can grow in Falmouth's gardens, and why the grass and trees here stay green for so much of the year.
