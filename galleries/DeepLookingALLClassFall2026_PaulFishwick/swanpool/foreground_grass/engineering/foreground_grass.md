Before 1830, lawns were cut by people swinging scythes, or kept short by grazing sheep. Then Edwin Budding, an engineer in Gloucestershire, England, invented the lawn mower. He got the idea from a machine used in cloth mills that trimmed the fuzz off fabric with a spinning cylinder of blades. Budding's mower used a similar cylinder that cut grass like scissors as it was pushed along. The neat, even grass in Pamela's photo is the result of his invention and nearly 200 years of improvements.
