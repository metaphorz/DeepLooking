For centuries in England, some land was not owned outright by one person but shared. On common land, local people had rights to graze animals, cut turf for fuel or gather wood. Over time, much of this land was fenced off and turned into private farms. Some open spaces, especially on cliffs and coasts, were later protected for everyone to enjoy, often by local councils or the National Trust. A wide clifftop lawn like this one, used for walking, picnics and play, carries on that old idea of shared green space.
