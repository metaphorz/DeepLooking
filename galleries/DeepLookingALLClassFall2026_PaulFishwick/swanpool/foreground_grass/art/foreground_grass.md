In 1503, the German artist Albrecht Dürer painted a small watercolor of an ordinary clump of grass and weeds, as if seen by someone lying on the ground. It is called the Great Piece of Turf. Dürer painted every blade, stem and leaf so carefully that botanists can still name the plants. He showed that even the plainest corner of nature is worth looking at closely. Try it with the grass in this photo: from far away it is one green field, but up close it would be thousands of separate plants.
