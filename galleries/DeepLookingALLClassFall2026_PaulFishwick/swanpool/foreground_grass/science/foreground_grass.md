Most plants grow from the tips of their stems, so cutting off the top stops them. Grasses are different. Their growing point sits low, near the soil, and new leaf tissue is pushed up from the base of each blade. That is why grass survives being grazed by animals or cut by a mower and grows straight back. Grasses belong to a huge plant family, the Poaceae, which also includes wheat, rice and corn. On a windy, salty clifftop like this one, low growth also helps grass survive the weather.
