In the 1800s, the Fox family, Quaker merchants and shipping agents in Falmouth, asked the captains of ships arriving from around the world to bring back plants and seeds. Thanks to Cornwall's mild climate, many of these plants thrived. Alfred Fox created Glendurgan Garden on the nearby Helford River in the 1820s, including a laurel maze planted in 1833 that visitors still walk today. Some of the trees and shrubs around Falmouth's coast are descendants of plants carried home by ships, part of a long history of plant collecting.
