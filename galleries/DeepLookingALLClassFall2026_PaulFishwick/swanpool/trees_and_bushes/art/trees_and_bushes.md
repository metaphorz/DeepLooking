In the 1880s, a group of artists settled in the Cornish fishing village of Newlyn. Led by painters such as Stanhope Forbes, they worked plein air, a French term for painting in the open air, setting up easels on beaches, in lanes and among the trees instead of in a studio. They wanted to capture real daylight and the grey, green and silver tones of the Cornish coast. Look at the many greens in the trees and bushes here, from dark pine to bright leaves in sun. How many different greens would a painter need?
