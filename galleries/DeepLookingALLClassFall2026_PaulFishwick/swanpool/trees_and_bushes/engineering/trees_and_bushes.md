On an exposed coast, strong winds blow salt spray inland and can damage crops, gardens and buildings. Farmers and gardeners plant rows of tough trees and shrubs as a windbreak. A windbreak does not stop the wind completely; it slows it down and lets some air filter through, which works better than a solid wall that causes swirling gusts. The sheltered area can reach about ten times the height of the trees downwind. The belts of trees and bushes in this photo help shelter the fields and houses behind the shore.
