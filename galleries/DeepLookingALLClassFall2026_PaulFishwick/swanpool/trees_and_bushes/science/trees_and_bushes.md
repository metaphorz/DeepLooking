Trees on windy coasts often lean away from the sea, with their branches bunched on one side. The wind and salt spray dry out and kill the buds facing the ocean, while buds on the sheltered side keep growing. Over many years, this pruning shapes the tree like a flag. In very harsh places, such as mountains near the tree line, trees can be stunted into low, twisted shapes called krummholz, a German word for crooked wood. Look at the edges of the trees here. Do they look shaped by the wind?
