Sunlight contains all the colors of the rainbow. When it passes through the air, tiny gas molecules scatter short blue wavelengths much more than long red ones. That scattered blue light comes at us from every direction, so the sky looks blue. This is called Rayleigh scattering. Cloud droplets are much bigger than gas molecules, and they scatter all colors about equally, so clouds look white. The thicker parts of a cloud let less light through, which is why their bases look grey. Pamela Gray's photo shows all three: blue sky, white tops and grey bases.
