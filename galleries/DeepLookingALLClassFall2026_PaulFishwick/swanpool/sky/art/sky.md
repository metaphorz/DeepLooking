In 1821 and 1822, the English painter John Constable made dozens of quick oil sketches of nothing but clouds. He called it skying. On the back of each one he wrote the date, the time of day and the direction of the wind, almost like a scientist keeping records. Constable believed the sky was the key to a landscape painting, because it sets the light and mood for everything below it. The heaped white clouds over the Cornish coast here would have been perfect subjects for one of his sketches.
