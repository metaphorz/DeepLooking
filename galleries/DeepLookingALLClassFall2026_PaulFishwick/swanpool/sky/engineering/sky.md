A short drive south of Falmouth, on the Lizard peninsula, stands the Goonhilly Satellite Earth Station. In July 1962 its great dish antenna, nicknamed Arthur, received some of the first live television pictures sent across the Atlantic by the satellite Telstar. Engineers had to point the huge dish precisely and track the satellite as it raced across the sky. The open skies over this part of Cornwall, far from big cities, made it a good place to listen to space. Goonhilly's dishes still track spacecraft today.
