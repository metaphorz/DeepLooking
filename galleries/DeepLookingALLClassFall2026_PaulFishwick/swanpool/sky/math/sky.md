You can estimate how high the bottoms of these clouds are with a simple rule. Rising air cools, and clouds form when it cools to its dew point, the temperature at which water vapor condenses. For puffy clouds like these, the cloud base is roughly 125 meters higher for every degree Celsius between the air temperature and the dew point at the ground. If it is 20 degrees and the dew point is 12, the gap is 8 degrees, and 8 times 125 is 1,000 meters. Try it with today's weather.
