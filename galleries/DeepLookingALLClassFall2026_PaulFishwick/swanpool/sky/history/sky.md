On 12 December 1901, a signal sent from Poldhu, on the Cornish coast south of Falmouth, crossed the Atlantic Ocean without any wires. Guglielmo Marconi, waiting in Newfoundland with an antenna held up by a kite, heard three faint clicks, the letter S in Morse code. Many scientists had said radio waves could not follow the curve of the Earth. Marconi showed they could, and long-distance radio was born. The same sky you see in this photo carried that first transatlantic message.
