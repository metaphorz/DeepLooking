The great harbor at Falmouth, called Carrick Roads, is a ria, a river valley drowned by the sea. At the end of the last Ice Age, melting ice sheets around the world raised sea level by more than 100 meters. The sea flooded into the low river valleys of Cornwall, making long, branching inlets with deep water. Unlike a Norwegian fjord, which was carved by a glacier, a ria was carved by a river and then filled by the rising sea. That deep water is why Falmouth became such an important port.
