From 1689 to the mid 1800s, Falmouth was home to the Packet Service, fast sailing ships that carried mail between Britain and Spain, Portugal, the West Indies and the Americas. Falmouth was chosen because it is near the western tip of England, so ships reached the open ocean quickly. In 1805, a small ship called HMS Pickle landed at Falmouth with the news of the victory at Trafalgar and the death of Admiral Nelson, and its officer raced by coach to London. The waters in this photo were once a gateway to the world.
