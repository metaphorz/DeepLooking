Cornish buildings need to keep out rain blown in from the Atlantic. Slate is ideal for roofs because it splits into thin, flat sheets that are strong and waterproof and last for a century or more. Slate has been quarried at Delabole in north Cornwall since the 1400s, and its huge pit is one of the deepest quarries in England. Builders lay the slates in overlapping rows, nailed to wooden battens, so water runs off each one onto the next. Many coastal houses use thick stone walls too, painted white to reflect light and seal out damp.
