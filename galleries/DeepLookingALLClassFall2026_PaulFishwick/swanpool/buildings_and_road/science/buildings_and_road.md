Buildings near the sea face an unusual enemy: salt. Wind carries salt spray onshore, and when the water dries, salt crystals form inside tiny pores in stone, brick and mortar. As the crystals grow, they push outward and slowly crack the material apart. Geologists call this salt weathering, or haloclasty. Salt also speeds up rust on metal railings and car bodies. That is why coastal buildings need regular painting and repair. The white walls in this photo may be protecting the stone underneath from the salty air.
