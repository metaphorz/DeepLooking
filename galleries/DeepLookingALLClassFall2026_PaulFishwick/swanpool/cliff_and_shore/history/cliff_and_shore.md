In the 1700s, high taxes on goods such as brandy, tea and tobacco made smuggling a big business in Cornwall. Small boats slipped into quiet coves at night to land goods without paying duty, and whole communities sometimes helped hide them. Smugglers called themselves free traders. The most famous, John Carter, was known as the King of Prussia and operated from a cove on Mount's Bay. Coastguards were later stationed along the coast to stop them, and the paths they walked became part of today's coastal footpath.
