Cliffs are slowly worn away by waves, rain and frost. Geologists measure how fast by comparing old maps and photographs with new ones. Suppose a cliff edge has moved 3 meters inland over 60 years. Its average rate of retreat is 3 divided by 60, or 5 centimeters per year. At that rate, how far would it move in a century? Hard rock may wear back only a few millimeters a year, while soft clay cliffs can lose a meter or more in a single storm. The rock type matters.
