The cliffs around Falmouth are mostly made of slates and sandstones from the Devonian period, roughly 400 to 360 million years ago, when this area lay under an ancient sea. Mud and sand settled in layers and hardened into rock. Later, when molten granite pushed up from below to form much of Cornwall's high ground, the heat and pressure hardened these rocks further. Miners called them killas. Look at the brownish face of the cliff in the photo. Can you see layers, cracks or places where the sea has cut in?
