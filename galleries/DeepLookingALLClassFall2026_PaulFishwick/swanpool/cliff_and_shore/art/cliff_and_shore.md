Charles Napier Hemy settled in Falmouth in the 1880s and became one of the best-known marine painters of his time. To study the sea up close, he worked from a boat fitted out as a floating studio, so he could paint waves, rocks and fishermen from the water. His paintings show the weight and movement of the sea against the Cornish shore. Notice the line where the pale beach and grey-brown cliff meet the water in Pamela Gray's photo. Hemy spent his life trying to paint edges like this one.
