The sky fills about two thirds of the picture, and the horizon sits at a fixed height, a simple way to divide a scene into proportions. Mathematicians describe the sky's color shift as a gradient, a smooth change in value across space. Brightness drops gradually from the horizon upward. The horizon is also a geometry problem: for a viewer standing about 20 meters above the sea on a ship, the distance to the horizon is roughly the square root of 2 times Earth's radius times height, about 16 kilometers. That is why distant mountains on the left appear to rise from the water in a gentle curve.
