The sky's color comes from Rayleigh scattering. Air molecules scatter short blue wavelengths of sunlight much more than long red ones. After sunset, direct sunlight no longer reaches us, but sunlight still lights the upper atmosphere and is scattered down as blue light. Ozone in the stratosphere also absorbs orange and red light, which deepens the blue during twilight. The sky is darker at the top because the light path there is different, and it is brighter near the horizon, where the sun's glow lingers. In December at this latitude, twilight is fairly short, so the blue hour passes quickly.
