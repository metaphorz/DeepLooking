For thousands of years, sailors along Turkey's Aegean coast read the evening sky to guide their journeys. Ancient Greeks, Phoenicians, and later Ottoman and Genoese mariners watched where stars first appeared and judged the weather from sunsets. The Kusadasi area, once the Greek and Roman region near Ephesus, was a busy trading coast. Its castle on Pigeon Island was fortified in the Ottoman period, and lookouts there scanned the same horizon for approaching ships. Dusk was a risky time, when visibility dropped and vessels needed safe harbor. Today's cruise passengers watch the same sky, but with modern lights and charts.
