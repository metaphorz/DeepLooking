Physics explains why these boats rest calmly on the dark water. Buoyancy is the upward push from a fluid, and it grows with the volume of water a hull displaces. A steel or aluminum ferry is much denser than water, but its hollow shape holds so much air that its average density is lower than the sea. That is why it floats. Passengers add weight, so the hull sinks a little deeper until the forces balance again. The harbor is sheltered, so the water looks smooth, and the boats stay level because their center of gravity is low compared with their wide hull.
