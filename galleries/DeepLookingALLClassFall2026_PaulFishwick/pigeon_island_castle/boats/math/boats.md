A boat floats because of a simple numerical rule. Archimedes' principle says the upward force on a hull equals the weight of the water it pushes aside. Designers measure this as displacement, so a boat weighing 50 tons must displace 50 tons of water, about 50 cubic meters of fresh water. Hull length and width give ratios that predict speed and stability. A long, narrow hull cuts through waves with less drag, while a wide one is steadier. Notice how the two small ferries are long compared with their width. Their repeated rows of windows also form a regular pattern, showing how repetition and proportion shape design.
