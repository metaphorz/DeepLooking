Boats have connected Kusadasi to the sea for centuries. The town sits on Turkey's Aegean coast near ancient Ephesus, and its harbor grew as a trading port under the Ottoman Empire, when merchants shipped goods such as figs, olives, and cotton. The fortified island behind the ferries, known as Pigeon Island, was linked to the shore by a causeway, and its walls guarded the anchorage. Today the same waters welcome cruise ships and small passenger boats like the two white ones here. The old fortress and modern ferries show how a harbor keeps serving travelers across many eras.
