These white piers look like floating pontoons, hollow or foam-filled sections that ride on the water instead of standing on fixed legs. This design lets the deck rise and fall with tides and waves, so ships can tie up at a steady height. Chains or piles hold the pontoons in place so they do not drift. The dark round fenders along the edge are shock absorbers, usually rubber, which protect both the hull and the pier when a ship bumps against it. The yellow post on the right is likely a navigation marker, warning boats where the harbor structure ends.
