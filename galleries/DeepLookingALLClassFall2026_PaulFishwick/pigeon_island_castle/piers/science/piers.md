Floating piers work because of buoyancy, explained by Archimedes' principle: water pushes up on an object with a force equal to the weight of the water it displaces. The pier's hollow, air-filled or foam-filled pontoons displace a lot of water compared with their weight, so they float. The calm, dark sea in the photo also shows how the Aegean surface looks at dusk, when low light and the water's absorption of red wavelengths make it appear deep blue. Small waves and tides move the water, but the pier floats up and down while staying in position.
