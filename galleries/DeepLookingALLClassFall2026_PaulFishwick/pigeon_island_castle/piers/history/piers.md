Kusadasi sits near ancient Ephesus, once a major Greek and Roman port that slowly silted up. As Ephesus declined, nearby coastal towns grew, and Kusadasi became a trading harbor under the Byzantines, Genoese, and later the Ottomans. The fortress on Pigeon Island was fortified in the Ottoman period, with walls enclosing the small rock. Modern piers like these in the foreground were added when Kusadasi became one of Turkey's busiest cruise ports, linking the old Aegean trade route to present-day tourism. Visitors now arrive by ship, much as merchants and pilgrims did for centuries.
