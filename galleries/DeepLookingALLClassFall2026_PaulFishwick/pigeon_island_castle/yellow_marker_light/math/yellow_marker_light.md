The yellow marker is a narrow vertical rectangle, roughly a line segment standing on a long horizontal pier. The two lines meet at nearly a right angle, or 90 degrees, which is a basic geometric relationship in design. In the photograph, the marker sits about three quarters of the way across the frame. This is close to the rule of thirds, which divides a picture into a three by three grid and places key objects near the lines. Mathematicians would also note the pattern of the pier's evenly spaced fenders, which repeat at regular intervals, and the way the horizon forms a nearly perfect straight line.
