At dusk, your eyes shift from using cone cells, which see color in bright light, to rod cells, which work better in dim light. This change is called the Purkinje effect. Even so, a bright yellow lamp stays easy to see against the deep blue sky because it contrasts strongly with the background. The marker also emits light itself, while the water mostly reflects what little sky light remains. Blue wavelengths scatter more in the atmosphere, which helps explain the sky's rich blue color after sunset. Yellow light travels through haze and sea mist fairly well, making it practical for marking harbor hazards.
