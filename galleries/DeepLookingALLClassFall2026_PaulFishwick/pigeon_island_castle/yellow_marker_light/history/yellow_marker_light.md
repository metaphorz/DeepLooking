Kusadasi has been a port for centuries, and the fortified Pigeon Island behind the marker reminds us why. Its walls were built to guard the harbor, and the town grew as a trading stop near ancient Ephesus. Sailors once relied on fires, torches, and landmarks to find safe entry at night. The modern yellow marker continues that old tradition with electric light. Today cruise ships, like the one from which this photograph was taken, bring visitors in December and every season. The marker links past and present, showing how ports have always needed clear signals to welcome travelers and protect ships from rocks and shallow water.
