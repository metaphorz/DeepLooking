The yellow marker is an aid to navigation, a device engineered to guide ships safely. It stands on a concrete pier built from large floating or fixed blocks, with rubber fenders along the edge to absorb the bumps of boats. Engineers choose a tall, slim tower so the light can be seen from far away, and they use bright LEDs or lamps with a lens to focus the beam. The structure must resist salt water, wind, and waves, so it uses corrosion-resistant materials and a sealed power system. Its color and flash pattern follow international rules, which lets any captain interpret it quickly at dusk.
