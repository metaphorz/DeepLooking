The island is a small rocky outcrop of the Aegean coast, a region stretched and broken by tectonic forces. Its pale walls were likely built from local limestone, a sedimentary rock made mostly of calcium carbonate from ancient marine organisms. Light-colored stone reflects a lot of light, which is why the walls glow so strongly when lit. The orange color comes from the lamps' warm spectrum, while the sky shows the deep blue of twilight, when sunlight is scattered and only dim light remains. Salt spray and waves slowly dissolve and weather limestone, so coastal walls need repair. The calm, dark water reflects very little light, making the wall stand out.
