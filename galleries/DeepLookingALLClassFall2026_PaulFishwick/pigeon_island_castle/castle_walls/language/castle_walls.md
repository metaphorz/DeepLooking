The glowing walls sit on Güvercin Adası, Turkish for Pigeon Island. Kuşadası means bird island: kuş is bird and ada is island, with the suffix -sı marking possession. Güvercin means pigeon or dove, so the island carries two bird names. Some writers link the town's name to Italian merchants, who may have called it Scala Nova, or New Landing, while the Byzantine Greeks used Ephesian place names. Turkish is agglutinative, meaning endings are stacked onto root words, and the name Kuşadası shows how roots join into a compact label. The word kale, meaning castle, comes from Latin castellum through Arabic and Persian routes, like many fortress words across languages.
