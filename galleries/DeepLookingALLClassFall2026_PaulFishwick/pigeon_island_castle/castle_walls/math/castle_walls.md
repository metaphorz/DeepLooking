The photograph is built from stacked horizontal bands: sky, glowing wall, dark hill, pier, and water. The bright wall sits close to a horizontal third line of the frame, a composition guideline called the rule of thirds. The wall's top is a nearly straight line, while the rock below it is irregular, a contrast between Euclidean geometry and natural shapes. The wall is made of repeating rectangles and small projecting towers, a rhythm of translation symmetry. We can also estimate size with similar triangles: if the wall is about 200 meters away and appears a certain angular width, simple proportions give its real length. Perspective makes the far end seem shorter.
