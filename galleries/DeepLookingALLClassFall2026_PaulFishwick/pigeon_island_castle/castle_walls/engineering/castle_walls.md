The castle walls show how military engineers used the terrain. Building on a steep rocky island gave the defenders height and made attack by sea difficult. The thick stone curtain wall follows the shape of the hill, and the flat, crenellated top provided a walkway where defenders could stand behind protective gaps. Builders used local stone and mortar, which resist compression well but need thick sections to survive impact. The walls also had to stand up to salt spray and earthquakes, common in western Turkey. Today another kind of engineering is visible: LED or floodlight fixtures mounted along the base light the stone evenly while limiting glare. A modern causeway and harbor piers now serve the nearby cruise port.
