Pigeon Island's fortress is tied to centuries of Aegean power struggles. The island has been fortified since Byzantine times, and Ottoman rulers rebuilt it in the 1500s and 1600s to protect the harbor and trade routes from pirates and rival navies. Nearby Ephesus had once been a great port, but its harbor silted up, so coastal towns like Kuşadası grew in importance. In the twentieth century the island was joined to the mainland by a causeway, and the castle was restored for visitors. Today cruise ships like the one that carried this photographer stop here, so an old defensive site now welcomes travelers instead of guarding against them.
