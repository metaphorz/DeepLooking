At dusk our eyes shift from color-sensitive cones toward rod cells, which work better in dim light. Scientists call this the Purkinje effect. Blues look brighter and reds look darker as the light fades. That helps explain why the sky and sea look deep blue while the tower glows. The tower seems white because floodlights are far brighter than the leftover daylight, and the camera records them as nearly white. The wall's yellow light comes from a different lamp color. The tower also stands on a rocky hill, where weathering by wind, salt, and waves has shaped the stone.
