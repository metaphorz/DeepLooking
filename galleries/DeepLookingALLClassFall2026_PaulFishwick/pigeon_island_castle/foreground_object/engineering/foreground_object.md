If this foreground object is a mooring fitting, it belongs to a system that holds a ship against wind and current. A rope, called a line, loops around a strong post and carries the load into the ship's hull or the dock. Engineers design these fittings from cast steel or iron, with rounded tops so lines do not snag or fray. Fittings are rated for a safe working load, based on the ship's mass and the strongest wind it might face. In the background, the concrete breakwaters have black rubber fenders along their sides. These soak up the energy when a vessel touches the pier, so neither the hull nor the concrete is damaged.
