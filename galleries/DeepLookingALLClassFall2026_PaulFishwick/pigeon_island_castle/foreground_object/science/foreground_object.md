The scene is lit by twilight, when the Sun is below the horizon but its light is still scattered by the upper atmosphere. Blue wavelengths scatter most, which is why the sky and sea look deep navy. The pale foreground object stands out because its smooth, light-colored surface reflects more of this faint light than the dark water does. The castle walls glow yellow because they are lit by artificial floodlights, not the Sun. Our eyes also change at dusk. Rod cells in the retina take over from cone cells, so colors fade and brightness differences become more noticeable. That is why a small pale shape can seem so bright in the photo.
