At the bottom edge of the photo sits a pale, rounded fixture, most likely a mooring post or ship fitting. It is hard to be sure at dusk. If it is a bollard, its name has an interesting past. Linguists think the word comes from the Middle English and Old Norse idea of a bole, meaning a thick tree trunk, because early posts for tying ships were short, stout logs. The ending -ard is a common suffix in English, as in drunkard or wizard, and often marks a person or thing with a strong trait. Nearby, the Turkish place name Kuşadası means Bird Island, which fits the old Pigeon Island nickname. Words for harbor objects often record what the objects were first made from.
