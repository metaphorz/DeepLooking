The foreground object looks like the top of a rounded form. Its edge appears as a shallow arc, much like a spherical cap, the piece you get when a plane slices off the top of a sphere. A cap is described by two measurements, its height and the radius of its base. From those you can calculate its curved surface area with the formula 2 times pi times the sphere radius times the cap height. The curve matters for another reason: it reflects light unevenly, so the brightest patch sits where the surface faces the light most directly. In contrast, the breakwaters in the distance are made of straight lines and right angles, so the picture sets rounded geometry against rectangular geometry.
