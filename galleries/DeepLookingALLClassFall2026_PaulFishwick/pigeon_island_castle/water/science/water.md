The water looks blue because of physics. Water absorbs red, orange, and yellow light more strongly than blue, so light that travels into the sea and scatters back to us is mostly blue. At dusk the sky is dim and dark, and the sea reflects that sky as well, deepening the color. The small ripples come from wind transferring energy to the surface, forming capillary and gravity waves. The Aegean is part of the Mediterranean, which is salty because evaporation is high in a warm climate. Salinity, temperature, and tiny particles like plankton all change how clear and how blue the water appears.
