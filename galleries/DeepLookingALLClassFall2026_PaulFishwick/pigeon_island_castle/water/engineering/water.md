This water is a working harbor, shaped by engineering. The long concrete structures are piers and breakwaters, built from large blocks to absorb wave energy and make a calmer basin where ships can dock. Rubber fenders, the dark rounded shapes along the edges, cushion hulls as they touch the pier. The yellow post marks the end of a pier so that pilots can navigate safely at dusk. Engineers must know the depth, the tide range, and the typical wave size to size these structures. The Mediterranean has small tides, so designers focus mainly on wind waves and the weight of large cruise ships.
