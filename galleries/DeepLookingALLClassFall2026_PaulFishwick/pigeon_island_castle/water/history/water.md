The Aegean has linked peoples for thousands of years. Greek colonists, Persians, Romans, Byzantines, and Ottomans all sailed these waters. Nearby Ephesus was once a great port, until silt from the river slowly filled its harbor and left it inland, showing how the sea's edge can shift. Kusadasi grew as Ephesus declined, and its small island fortress, which was fortified in the Ottoman era, guarded the approach by water. Genoese and Venetian merchants also traded here. Today the same sea carries cruise ships like the one that carried the photographer, turning an old trade route into a path for visitors in December 2024.
