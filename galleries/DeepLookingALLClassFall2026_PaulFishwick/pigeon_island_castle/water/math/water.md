The sea gives a clean example of geometry. The horizon is almost a straight line because Earth's curvature is very gentle. For an observer standing h meters above the water, the distance to the horizon is roughly 3.57 times the square root of h in kilometers. From a ship deck about 15 meters up, that is around 14 kilometers. The ripples show pattern and periodicity: repeated crests separated by a wavelength. Mathematicians model them with sine functions, where amplitude gives wave height and frequency tells how often crests pass. Perspective also shrinks the ripples toward the distance, a visual example of how spacing changes with depth.
