Artists have painted seascapes for centuries, and this scene shows why. The water fills the lower half of the frame in layered blues, from bright turquoise near the viewer to darker tones near the horizon. Small ripples catch the last light and make a soft texture, like brushwork. Painters call this kind of mood a nocturne when dusk dominates, as in the works of James McNeill Whistler. Notice the color contrast too: the cool blue sea sets off the warm golden walls of the fortress, which are opposite each other on the color wheel. That contrast pulls the eye from the water toward the island and gives the picture its drama.
