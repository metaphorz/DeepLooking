At dusk, the trees on the island become a ragged dark edge sitting on top of the lit golden walls. Artists call this kind of strong light-and-dark contrast chiaroscuro. Here the foliage is not shown leaf by leaf; the eye reads it as one lumpy mass, like a painter's quick brushwork. The warm orange of the lit stone sits against the cool navy sky, a classic complementary color pairing that makes each look stronger. The trees also soften the hard, straight line of the battlements, giving the composition a organic crown. Photographers and painters often use this trick, placing a rough natural shape over a geometric one to add visual interest.
