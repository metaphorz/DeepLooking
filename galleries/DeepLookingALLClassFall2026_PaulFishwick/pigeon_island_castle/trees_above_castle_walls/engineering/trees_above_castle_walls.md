Trees above castle walls raise a real engineering problem. Roots search for water and push into cracks in stone and mortar, slowly widening them as they thicken. Over decades this can loosen blocks, let rainwater in, and weaken the wall. Heritage engineers manage this by cutting back vegetation, treating stumps, and repairing joints with lime mortar that matches the original. The castle's thick walls, built on a rocky islet, were designed to resist cannon fire and waves, not roots. Still, the trees also help by holding soil on the slopes and reducing erosion around the foundation, so the engineer must balance protection and preservation.
