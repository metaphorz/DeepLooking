The treetops form an uneven outline above the straight wall, and that contrast is a math lesson. The wall is made of nearly straight line segments, simple geometry with a clear length and angle. The tree edge is closer to a fractal: branches split into smaller branches, which split again, so the outline looks rough at almost every scale. Mathematicians measure this roughness with fractal dimension, a number between a smooth line (1) and a filled area (2). A tree crown's edge would fall somewhere between them. Even from far away, you can estimate heights by comparing the trees to the wall using ratios, a method called similar triangles.
