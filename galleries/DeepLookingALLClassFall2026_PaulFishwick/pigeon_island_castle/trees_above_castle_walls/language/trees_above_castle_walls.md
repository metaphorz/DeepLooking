The trees above the castle walls are named differently depending on who is speaking. English 'tree' comes from an old Germanic root related to 'true' and 'firm,' since trees were seen as solid and steady. In Turkish, the word is ağaç, and a grove or woodland is orman. Turkish is an agglutinative language, meaning it builds meaning by stacking suffixes: ağaçlar means 'trees,' and ağaçlarda means 'on the trees.' The island itself is called Güvercin Adası, 'Pigeon Island,' so the whole scene is labeled in a language that glues small pieces together. Even the English word 'castle' traces back to Latin castellum, a small fortified place.
