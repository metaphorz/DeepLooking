The castle walls under these trees were built on a small rocky islet that is now joined to the mainland by a causeway. The fortress is often dated to the Byzantine era, then rebuilt by the Ottomans in the 1600s to guard the harbor against pirates. It also served as a lookout and refuge. The island's name, Güvercin Adası, means Pigeon Island, and the trees and gardens that grew up inside the walls came much later. Today the site is a park with cafes and a tower, and its lit walls greet cruise ships that visit the Aegean port of Kuşadası, near ancient Ephesus.
