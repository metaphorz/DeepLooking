The trees on this small island grow in a Mediterranean climate: mild, wet winters and hot, dry summers. Plants here tend to be tough, with small leathery leaves that lose less water. Species like pines, olives, and shrubs such as maquis thrive on thin rocky soil. On an exposed islet, constant sea wind and salt spray stunt growth and shape the crowns into low, dense masses, which is why the canopy looks compact rather than tall. Photosynthesis still works at dusk only weakly, since light is fading. Trees also cool the ground through transpiration, releasing water vapor from their leaves.
