The blue tones come from physics. After sunset, sunlight reaches the sky only indirectly, and the atmosphere scatters short blue wavelengths more than red ones, a process called Rayleigh scattering. Water adds to the effect by absorbing red light and reflecting the blue sky, so the Aegean looks deep navy. Against this cool background, the artificial lamps on the castle emit warm, sodium-like light, which looks even more orange to our eyes. The rocky island itself is a small piece of the coastline, made of resistant rock that stood above the water as the shoreline changed over time.
