The engineering challenge of hanging small birds is deceptively simple: the string must hold the bird's weight without tearing the delicate skin, while allowing air circulation for the faisandage (aging) process.

The strings in Cotán's painting appear to be linen thread — the standard cordage material in 17th-century Castile. Linen thread has a tensile strength of approximately 50-100 MPa, meaning even a single thread could support several kilograms. The engineering constraint is not strength but friction: the knot around the bird's leg must grip without slipping, yet the bird's leg is smooth and tapers.

The solution was a slip knot or clove hitch tied above the tarsometatarsus (the scaled "ankle" joint), where the natural widening of the joint prevents the loop from sliding off. This is the same principle used in modern surgical ligatures — the anatomy provides the mechanical stop.
