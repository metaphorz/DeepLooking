title: Geometry of the Hollow Cylinder
url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cylinder
keyword: cylinder

The bamboo cane is a near-perfect hollow cylinder — one of the most structurally efficient geometries in nature. Its strength-to-weight ratio derives from concentrating material at the outer radius, where resistance to bending is greatest.

The moment of inertia for a hollow cylinder is I = π(R⁴ - r⁴)/4, where R is the outer radius and r is the inner radius. For bamboo, where the wall thickness is typically 10-15% of the diameter, this yields approximately 85% of the strength of a solid rod at only 30% of the weight.

The visible nodes (septa) along the cane divide it into segments, each acting as a reinforced compartment. This segmented design prevents catastrophic buckling — a crack in one segment cannot propagate past the node. Engineers call this "damage tolerance through compartmentalization."
