In Spanish, hunting vocabulary distinguishes between "caza mayor" (big game — deer, boar) and "caza menor" (small game — birds, rabbits). The eight small birds in Cotán's painting belong firmly to caza menor.

According to the Museo del Prado's catalogue, the painting contains three species: "verdecillos" (European Serins, Serinus serinus), "jilgueros" (European Goldfinches, Carduelis carduelis), and "gorriones" (House Sparrows, Passer domesticus). "Jilguero" comes from Latin "carduelis," meaning "thistle-eater" — the same root as "cardoon," another object in this very painting.

"Verdecillo" means "little green one," a diminutive of "verde" (green), describing the serin's olive-green plumage. "Gorrión" derives from a pre-Roman Iberian root, suggesting the house sparrow's intimate association with human settlements predates recorded history. The verb "cazar" itself comes from Vulgar Latin "captiare" (to capture), the same root that gives English "catch."
