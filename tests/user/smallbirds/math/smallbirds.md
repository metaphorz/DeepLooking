title: Scaling Laws — Allometry in Small Birds
url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allometry
keyword: allometry

The three species in Cotán's painting span a range of sizes that illustrate allometric scaling laws. The European Serin weighs roughly 11-12 grams, the goldfinch 14-19 grams, and the house sparrow 24-40 grams — nearly a fourfold range.

For birds, metabolic rate scales as M^0.72 — meaning the tiny serin burns energy faster per gram than the larger sparrow. In life, the serin's heart rate would have been approximately 600-800 beats per minute, the sparrow's closer to 400-600.

Wing loading (weight divided by wing area) scales as M^0.28, explaining why the smallest serins can hover and maneuver in ways impossible for the larger sparrows. The allometric equation Y = aM^b relates body mass to nearly every biological function, revealing deep principles about how size constrains life.

Cotán arranged six birds on the cane in pairs — two of each species — creating a natural mathematical grouping that a Renaissance viewer would have recognized as orderly and deliberate.
