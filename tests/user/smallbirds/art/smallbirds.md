title: Vanitas and the Dead Bird — Mortality in Still Life

The eight small dead birds are Cotán's most explicit memento mori — a reminder of death that transforms this pantry scene into a meditation on mortality. They appear in two groups: six birds (two serins, two goldfinches, two sparrows) skewered on a bamboo cane resting on the ledge, and two more (a goldfinch and a sparrow) hanging from strings above.

In the tradition of vanitas painting, dead birds symbolize the fragility of life and the futility of earthly pleasures. Their small size amplifies this message: if something so light and quick can be stilled, how much more fragile are human ambitions?

Cotán renders their feathers with extraordinary precision — each barb and barbule visible in the raking light. The species are distinguishable: the goldfinch's red face patch, the serin's streaked green plumage, the sparrow's brown cap. This virtuoso technique serves a philosophical purpose: the more beautiful the rendering, the more poignant the reminder that beauty is transient.
