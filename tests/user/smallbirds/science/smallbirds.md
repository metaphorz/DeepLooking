title: Three Species — Serins, Goldfinches, and Sparrows
url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_serin
keyword: Serinus

The Museo del Prado identifies three species among Cotán's eight small birds: the European Serin (Serinus serinus), European Goldfinch (Carduelis carduelis), and House Sparrow (Passer domesticus). All three belong to different genera within the order Passeriformes.

The European Serin is the smallest European finch (11-12 cm), with streaked olive-green upperparts and a yellow rump. Its scientific name "serinus" derives from the French "serin," itself possibly from the Latin "sirena" — a reference to its persistent, buzzing song.

The European Goldfinch is unmistakable: a crimson face, black-and-white head, and golden wing bars make it one of the most colorful European passerines. Its specialized beak — longer and more pointed than other finches — evolved to extract seeds from thistles and teasels.

The House Sparrow, ubiquitous companion of human civilization, has followed agriculture across the globe. In Cotán's Toledo, sparrows nested in the very walls of houses and churches — making them the easiest of the three species to catch.
