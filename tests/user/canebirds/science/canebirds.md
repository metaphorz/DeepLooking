The six birds on the cane represent three species from two finch families, each occupying a distinct ecological niche in Cotán's Toledo.

The European Serin (Serinus serinus, family Fringillidae) is the smallest European finch at 11-12 cm. Its streaked olive-green upperparts and yellow rump distinguish it from its relatives. Serins favor open country with scattered trees — orchards, vineyards, and garden edges — making them abundant around Toledo's agricultural lands.

The European Goldfinch (Carduelis carduelis, family Fringillidae) is unmistakable: a crimson face, black-and-white head, and golden wing bars. Its specialized elongated beak evolved to extract seeds from thistles and teasels. Goldfinches gather in flocks called "charms" — from the Old English "cyrm" (noise) — and their gregarious feeding habits made them vulnerable to net traps.

The House Sparrow (Passer domesticus, family Passeridae) is a human commensal — a species that thrives in close association with people. In Toledo, sparrows nested in roof tiles, ate spilled grain, and were caught by boys with simple traps. They are the most common birds in the painting, just as they were in the city.
