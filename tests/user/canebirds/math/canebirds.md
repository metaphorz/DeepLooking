title: Combinatorics of the Cane — Pairs and Species
url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combinatorics
keyword: pairs

The six birds on the cane are arranged in three pairs — two serins, two goldfinches, two sparrows. This 2-2-2 grouping creates a natural combinatorial structure that a mathematically literate 17th-century viewer would have recognized as orderly.

The number of ways to arrange 6 birds of 3 species (2 identical per species) on a linear cane is 6!/(2!×2!×2!) = 90 distinct permutations. Cotán chose one specific arrangement — the particular stacking order he observed in the market or pantry.

The three species span a range of body masses: serins at 11-12g, goldfinches at 14-19g, sparrows at 24-40g. The total mass of six birds is approximately 100-160 grams — roughly one-third the weight of a single partridge. This scaling relationship illustrates how caza menor (small game) required volume to match the caloric yield of caza mayor (large game): you needed many small birds to equal one partridge.
