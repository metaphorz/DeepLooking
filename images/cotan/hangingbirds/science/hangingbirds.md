title: Goldfinch and Sparrow — Two Passerines Compared

The Museo del Prado identifies the two hanging birds as a European Goldfinch (Carduelis carduelis) and a House Sparrow (Passer domesticus). Both are passerines — perching birds of the order Passeriformes — but they belong to different families and occupy different ecological niches.

The goldfinch (family Fringillidae) is a specialist seed-eater with a uniquely elongated, pointed beak adapted for extracting seeds from thistle heads and teasels. Its crimson face, black-and-white head, and golden wing bars make it one of Europe's most recognizable birds. In life, it weighs 14-19 grams.

The house sparrow (family Passeridae) is a generalist granivore with a stout, conical beak suited for cracking a variety of seeds. It is one of the most successful bird species on Earth, having colonized every continent except Antarctica by following human agriculture. It weighs 24-40 grams — roughly twice the goldfinch.

Both species were year-round residents in Cotán's Toledo, making them available to hunters in all seasons.
