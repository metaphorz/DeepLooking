title: The Skewer — Ancient Food Technology
url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skewer
keyword: skewer

Threading small birds onto a rigid cane is one of humanity's oldest food engineering techniques. The cane serves simultaneously as storage rack, transport handle, counting device, and cooking spit — a single tool performing four functions.

The engineering is elegant: the cane's smooth outer surface (rich in silica) penetrates the birds' body cavities with minimal resistance. The hollow interior keeps the tool light. The nodes at regular intervals provide natural grip points and prevent birds from clustering at one end. The rigidity of the dried cane maintains spacing between birds, allowing air circulation that slows spoilage.

When ready to cook, the entire cane could be placed over a fire — the birds roasted in place without additional preparation. The cane itself, being green bamboo or Arundo donax, would char slowly rather than burn, maintaining structural integrity through the cooking process.

This technology appears unchanged in archaeological evidence spanning at least 3,000 years across the Mediterranean, from ancient Egyptian kitchen scenes to modern Spanish market practice.
