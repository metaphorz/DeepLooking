title: Giant Grass — The Biology of Bamboo

keyword: grass

Bamboo and its Mediterranean relative Arundo donax are both members of the grass family (Poaceae) — making this cane a close relative of wheat, rice, and corn. What distinguishes these "giant grasses" is their woody culm, which lignifies to produce a rigid, durable stem.

The green color visible in Cotán's painting indicates a freshly cut cane. The chlorophyll in the outer epidermis remains active for days after harvesting. As the cane dries, it transitions from green to golden-yellow — a process Cotán would have observed many times.

Arundo donax is one of the fastest-growing plants on Earth, capable of extending 10 cm per day under favorable conditions. A single rhizome can produce a dense stand of 6-meter canes in a single growing season, making it an effectively inexhaustible resource for the communities along the Tagus River near Toledo.
