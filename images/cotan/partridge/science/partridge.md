keyword: Alectoris

The two birds in Cotán's painting are Red-legged Partridges (Alectoris rufa), as confirmed by the Museo del Prado. This is the most common game bird of the Iberian Peninsula, identifiable by its distinctive blue-grey breast, chestnut-and-white barred flanks, red bill, and red legs.

Alectoris rufa is a gallinaceous bird (order Galliformes), more closely related to chickens and pheasants than to the serins, goldfinches, and sparrows hanging beside them. It is a ground-nesting species that prefers the dry, scrubby landscapes of Mediterranean Spain.

Red-legged partridges are unusual among birds for their double-clutch nesting strategy: females lay two clutches of 10-16 eggs each. The male incubates one clutch while the female incubates the other — a reproductive strategy that maximizes offspring in the harsh Iberian climate.

Each bird weighs 400-550 grams and can fly at speeds up to 58 km/h in short bursts. Their flight muscles constitute about 25% of body mass — reflecting the metabolic cost of their explosive takeoff, which evolved as an anti-predator defense.
